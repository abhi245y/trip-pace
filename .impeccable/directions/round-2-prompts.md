# Trip pace — round 2 direction candidates

Six worlds, deliberately spread across different material families so no two
look alike. Each carries an image-generation prompt to paste into ChatGPT.

Eliminated by the re-roll and not reusable: the Deck Log (now on branch
`design/deck-log`), tachograph chart, rally roadbook, avionics HUD, Swiss rail
timetable, Braun product, Indian milestone.

Binding constraint from the user: no didone display serif on the main UI.

Real content to keep in every image, so the comps are comparable:
present speed 104 km/h · moving avg 78 · trip avg 59 · max 96 ·
distance run 88.40 km · elapsed 1:30:07 · under way 1:08:20 · stopped 21:40 ·
remaining 64.9 km · arrive around 11:43 PM · destination Kozhikode ·
at present speed: 1.73 km/min, 52.0 km/30 min, 104.0 km/hour

## A — Tape Deck

A trip is a tape playing. Brushed aluminium fascia, warm amber backlight, VU
needles for pace against average, chunky transport controls. The roll assigned
this one.

**Prompt**

> A photorealistic product shot of a smartphone screen UI, portrait 9:19.5 aspect ratio, filling the frame edge to edge with no phone bezel or hand visible. The interface is designed as the fascia of a 1979 high-end cassette deck. Ground is brushed anodised aluminium in warm champagne grey (#b8b2a6) with fine horizontal grain, lit from the upper left so the grain catches. Recessed into the upper third is a black glass panel behind which a large amber-backlit numeric readout glows: "104" in a squarish technical sans with slightly rounded corners, roughly 40% of the screen width, in warm amber (#ffb03a) with a soft halo bloom on the black glass. Beneath it in small tracked capitals: "KM/H". To its right, two analogue VU meters with cream faces, black hairline arcs, printed red zones at the top of the scale, and thin black needles — the left meter labelled "MOVING AVG" reading 78, the right labelled "TRIP AVG" reading 59. Below the glass panel, a row of four rectangular transport buttons in dark grey plastic with chamfered edges and small engraved legends: STANDBY, START, RESET, and a smaller one. Below that, a recessed inset panel with a mechanical counter showing "88.40" in white drum digits on black, labelled "DISTANCE RUN KM". At the bottom, a strip of small backlit labels in the same amber: "REMAINING 64.9 KM", "ARRIVE 11:43 PM", "DESTINATION KOZHIKODE". Typography throughout is a technical sans in the manner of 1970s hi-fi silkscreen — no serifs anywhere, tight tracking on numbers, wide tracking on small capitals. Materials: brushed metal, black glass, dark plastic, cream meter faces. Lighting is a single soft warm source from the upper left with realistic specular highlights on the metal grain. No skeuomorphic wood veneer, no drop shadows floating in space, no glassmorphism, no gradients other than the metal's own sheen.

## B — Telemetry

The pit wall. Multi-channel traces over a black field, every channel a
different colour, the current value pinned to the right edge of its own trace.
Highest-resonance candidate on my list, and the most obvious one for a
speed product — which is exactly why the roll didn't assign it.

**Prompt**

> A smartphone screen UI, portrait 9:19.5 aspect ratio, filling the frame edge to edge, no phone bezel or hand. The interface is a motorsport telemetry readout on a pure black ground (#000000). The top 45% of the screen is a live trace chart: three overlaid line graphs plotted left to right on a faint dark grey grid of hairlines, one channel in electric cyan (#00e5ff), one in magenta (#ff2d95), one in acid yellow (#e8ff00), each 2px, sharp, no anti-aliased glow, no fill under the curves. The cyan trace ends at the right edge at a pinned value box reading "104" in a condensed technical sans, white on cyan. Below the chart, a dense data block laid out as a strict two-column table with hairline rules in dark grey (#1a1a1a): rows reading "MOV AVG 78", "TRIP AVG 59", "MAX 96", "RUN 88.40 km", "REM 64.9 km", "ETA 23:43", each label in small condensed uppercase grey (#7a7a7a) and each value in white condensed sans with tabular figures, values right-aligned. A horizontal band across the middle shows six small sector blocks in a row, four filled cyan and two dark, labelled "SECTORS". At the bottom, three flat rectangular buttons outlined in 1px dark grey with condensed uppercase labels: STANDBY, START TRIP, RESET. Typography is entirely a condensed grotesque, uppercase, tight, with tabular numerals — no serifs, no rounded corners anywhere, everything on a strict grid. The aesthetic is instrument-grade and unglamorous: flat, matte, no gradients, no glow, no glassmorphism, no drop shadows, no 3D. Pure data density on black.

## C — Truck Livery

Your own road, painted. The hand-lettered rear of an Indian lorry: saturated
fields, ornate shadowed lettering, mirrored borders, marigold and vermilion.
The most culturally grounded of the six and by far the loudest.

**Prompt**

> A smartphone screen UI, portrait 9:19.5 aspect ratio, filling the frame edge to edge, no phone bezel or hand visible. The interface is designed as hand-painted Indian truck art in the style of the decorated rear panel of a Tata lorry. Ground is a deep saturated field divided into horizontal painted bands: a vermilion band (#d62828), a marigold band (#f4a300), a bottle-green band (#0f6b47), separated by narrow white and black pinstripes with small painted floral motifs at the band joins. The dominant element, centred in the upper half, is the number "104" hand-lettered at large scale in the ornate Indian signwriter's style: fat brush-drawn numerals in cream white with a hard black drop-shadow offset down-right and a thin vermilion outline, slightly irregular as if painted freehand. Beneath it, hand-lettered in a smaller signpainter's script: "km/h". Around the numeral, symmetrical painted ornament — lotus rosettes, paisley curls, small painted mirrors rendered as silver discs with highlight dots. A painted banner ribbon across the middle carries "KOZHIKODE 64.9 KM" in cream hand-lettering on green. Below, a row of three painted plaques with black grounds and cream lettering reading "RUN 88.40", "AVG 78", "MAX 96", each plaque edged with a painted scalloped border. At the bottom, a hand-painted strip in the manner of a truck's mudflap lettering reading "STANDBY · START TRIP · RESET" in bold cream capitals with black shadow. Everything must look painted by hand with visible brush texture and slight asymmetry, on painted sheet metal with subtle scuffs — not vector-clean, not digitally perfect. No photographic truck, no vehicle, no road: only the painted panel filling the frame as a UI. No gradients, no glassmorphism, no drop shadows other than the painted ones.

## D — Survey Sheet

An Ordnance Survey map sheet. Contour lines, grid squares, and a legend
panel in the margin. Quiet, technical, paper-coloured — the calmest of the
six and the only light-ground candidate.

**Prompt**

> A smartphone screen UI, portrait 9:19.5 aspect ratio, filling the frame edge to edge, no phone bezel or hand. The interface is designed as a topographic survey map sheet. Ground is warm map paper (#f2ece0) with a faint printed grain. Across the whole screen runs a pale blue-grey 1km grid of hairlines (#c3d0d8) with small grid reference numerals in the margins. The upper half carries fine orange-brown contour lines (#b07d4a) curving across the sheet, with one contour thickened as an index line carrying an inline elevation figure. Overprinted on this, in the map's own legend style, is the primary readout: "104" in a large but restrained cartographic sans (like a map's spot-height label, not a display face), in dark ink brown (#3a2f28), with "km/h" set small beside it. The route runs across the sheet as a bold vermilion line (#c8341f) with small tick marks at intervals and a labelled endpoint "KOZHIKODE". A legend panel occupies the lower third: a bordered rectangle with a hairline rule frame, its title "THIS PASSAGE" in small letterspaced capitals, containing a two-column key with tiny sample symbols in the left column and values in the right — "DISTANCE RUN 88.40 km", "UNDER WAY 1:08:20", "STOPPED 21:40", "REMAINING 64.9 km", "ARRIVE 23:43" — set in a small clean cartographic sans with tabular figures. Along the bottom edge, a scale bar with alternating black and white segments, and three bordered rectangles reading STANDBY, START TRIP, RESET in small letterspaced capitals. Everything must read as offset-printed cartography: flat spot colours, hairline rules, no gradients, no shadows, no glow, no 3D, nothing glossy. The palette is limited to map paper, ink brown, contour orange-brown, grid blue-grey and one vermilion.

**Note:** this is the only candidate with a light ground. On a phone mounted in a car at night that is a real cost — worth seeing before choosing.

## E — Transit Diagram

Vignelli's subway map logic applied to a trip. Thick strokes, pure flat
colour, 45° geometry, circles as stations. The most graphic and the most
legible at a glance of the six.

**Prompt**

> A smartphone screen UI, portrait 9:19.5 aspect ratio, filling the frame edge to edge, no phone bezel or hand. The interface is designed as a modernist transit diagram in the Vignelli tradition. Ground is a flat near-black charcoal (#141414). Running vertically down the left third of the screen is a single thick route line, 14px wide, in flat signal orange (#ff6b1a), with two 45-degree bends and no curves other than a fixed small radius at each bend. Along the line sit station markers: solid white circles with dark centres, one filled solid orange to mark current position, each labelled to the right in a clean geometric grotesque in white — "DEPARTED", "NOW", "KOZHIKODE". The right two-thirds carries the data, set as flat colour blocks with generous black gutters between them and absolutely no borders or rules: a large orange block containing "104" in white geometric sans at large scale with "KM/H" small beneath; a white block containing "64.9" in black with "KM REMAINING"; a mid-grey block containing "88.40" with "KM RUN"; a smaller block row with "78 AVG", "96 MAX", "23:43 ARRIVE". Every number is set in the same geometric grotesque with consistent tight tracking, uppercase labels only, no mixed case anywhere. At the bottom, three flat colour blocks as buttons reading STANDBY, START TRIP, RESET, one of them orange. The whole composition is built on a strict grid with only horizontal, vertical and 45-degree elements. Flat design only: no gradients, no shadows, no texture, no glow, no depth, no glassmorphism. Pure flat colour, geometric type, and generous negative space.

## F — Chronograph

A mechanical chronograph dial. Concentric scales, sub-dials, applied indices,
lume. Instrument-luxury rather than instrument-industrial.

**Prompt**

> A smartphone screen UI, portrait 9:19.5 aspect ratio, filling the frame edge to edge, no phone bezel or hand visible. The interface is designed as the dial of a mechanical chronograph wristwatch, scaled to fill a phone screen. Ground is a deep gunmetal sunburst dial (#1c2024) with fine radial brushing catching light from the top. Occupying the upper two-thirds, a large circular dial with a printed outer tachymeter scale in white minute markers and small numerals around its rim, applied polished metal hour indices, and at its centre the primary readout "104" in a crisp technical sans in luminous cream (#eae3d2), with "KM/H" below in tiny letterspaced capitals. Two smaller circular sub-dials sit within the main dial, each with its own concentric hairline ring and thin needle: the left labelled "MOVING AVG" reading 78, the right labelled "MAX" reading 96, both with cream printed scales on a slightly darker recessed ground. A curved date-window style aperture at the lower edge of the dial shows "88.40 KM" in white on black. Below the dial, on the gunmetal ground, a horizontal row of three engraved-look readouts separated by fine vertical hairlines: "REMAINING 64.9 KM", "UNDER WAY 1:08:20", "ARRIVE 23:43", each in small letterspaced cream capitals. At the very bottom, three pusher-style controls rendered as knurled metal rectangles with engraved legends STANDBY, START TRIP, RESET. Details: applied metal indices with real specular highlights, a faint pale-green lume glow on the primary numerals only, printed scales with hairline precision. Realistic dial photography lighting, single soft source from above. No leather strap, no watch case, no wrist, no photographic watch — only the dial face treated as a full-screen interface. No glassmorphism, no purple, no neon.
